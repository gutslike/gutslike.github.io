# Learning Go: My 3-Phase Plan

I am starting to learn Go today, and I want to build a solid understanding of the language without rushing through it. My approach is simple: first get a quick overview, then practice by building something small, and finally study the language in more depth.

## Phase 1: Get the big picture

I will begin with the "X in Y Minutes" guide for Go to understand the language at a high level. This first step is about getting a feel for the syntax, core concepts, and overall style of Go.

## Phase 2: Practice by building

After that, I plan to take on a hands-on challenge from CodeCrafters: build your own shell in Go. Working on a guided project like this should help me understand how the language works in real scenarios and how different concepts fit together.

## Phase 3: Study the details deeply

Once I have a basic grasp of the language, I will go through the Go roadmap on roadmap.sh in more detail. My goal is to fill in the gaps, understand the important concepts more clearly, and connect the beginner-level knowledge with the deeper ideas behind the language.

## Why this plan makes sense

This approach follows a pattern that feels effective to me:

1. First, I get the gist of the language.
2. Then, I try it out by building something with guidance.
3. After that, I study the details more thoroughly.
4. Finally, I move on to independent projects and experiment more freely.

Once I have gone through this process, I plan to explore more ideas from Build Your Own X and start building my own projects in Go.
